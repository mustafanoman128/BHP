**For the non-technical folks 🏡**

You're looking to buy or rent a home in Bengaluru. You don't know if the price being quoted is fair. You don't know the market. You don't have an insider.

Now you do.

Just enter the neighbourhood, size, number of bedrooms and bathrooms — and in seconds you get:

✅ An instant price estimate in Lakhs
✅ A realistic price range (so you know the upper and lower bounds)
✅ The price per square foot — the number every Indian buyer actually thinks in
✅ Whether that property is above or below the area average — so you know if you're getting a deal or overpaying
✅ An EMI calculator that shows your monthly repayment based on your loan terms
✅ A side-by-side comparison across up to 3 Bengaluru neighbourhoods — so you can decide which area gives you the most value

No estate agent. No spreadsheet. No guesswork.

---

**For the technical folks 💻**

The original tutorial gave me a solid foundation — a scikit-learn Linear Regression model trained on ~7,000 cleaned property listings, with one-hot encoding across 240 Bengaluru locations, achieving an **R² of 0.8496.**

But I didn't stop there.

With the help of **Claude Code**, I went significantly further:

🔧 Built a full Flask REST API from scratch
🎨 Redesigned the entire frontend — frosted glass UI, amber theme, fully mobile-responsive
🔍 Integrated Tom Select for a searchable dropdown across all 240 neighbourhoods
📊 Added a live animated bar chart for location comparison using parallel API calls
🧮 Built a live EMI calculator using the standard reducing-balance formula
🏘️ Added an area average context badge — computed from the CSV at server startup, cached in memory
🚀 Deployed to **Render** with a full CI/CD pipeline — every push to GitHub auto-deploys
📁 Wrote proper documentation: CLAUDE.md, PRD, README with badges, an interview prep guide

**Why does this matter?**

India's real estate market is one of the most opaque in the world. Prices vary wildly between neighbourhoods. Buyers — especially first-time buyers — are at a massive information disadvantage.

Data Science exists to close that gap.

This project is small. But the **idea** is big: that a well-trained model, properly deployed, can genuinely help real people make better financial decisions.

That's the kind of work I want to keep doing.

And this is just the beginning. 🌍

One of my biggest dreams is to expand this to **Pakistan's real estate market** — a market that is equally opaque, equally underserved by data, and where millions of buyers make some of the biggest financial decisions of their lives with almost no visibility. The same model, localised to Pakistani cities and neighbourhoods, could give buyers there the same clarity and confidence that this app gives to people in Bengaluru. That's a problem worth solving.
